# The 10 Principles

**The values that guide our community.**

---

The 10 Principles of Burning Man were originally written by Larry Harvey as guidelines for the Burning Man community. They are not rules, but ideals that inform our culture and decisions.

---

## Radical Inclusion

Anyone may be a part of Burning Man. We welcome and respect the stranger. No prerequisites exist for participation in our community.

**In governance, this means:**
- Our processes are accessible to newcomers
- We don't gatekeep based on tenure or status
- Documentation is public and understandable
- We actively lower barriers to participation

---

## Gifting

Burning Man is devoted to acts of gift giving. The value of a gift is unconditional. Gifting does not contemplate a return or an exchange for something of equal value.

**In governance, this means:**
- We share knowledge and resources freely
- Our work benefits the community, not individuals
- We recognize contributions without expectation of reward
- We create abundance, not scarcity

---

## Decommodification

In order to preserve the spirit of gifting, our community seeks to create social environments that are unmediated by commercial sponsorships, transactions, or advertising.

**In governance, this means:**
- Decisions aren't for sale
- We resist commercial influence
- Our identity isn't a brand to monetize
- Community benefit outweighs revenue potential

---

## Radical Self-Reliance

Burning Man encourages the individual to discover, exercise and rely on their inner resources.

**In governance, this means:**
- We provide frameworks, not answers
- We enable, don't do it for people
- We trust people to solve their own problems
- We offer tools, not dependencies

---

## Radical Self-Expression

Radical self-expression arises from the unique gifts of the individual. No one other than the individual or a collaborating group can determine its content. It is offered as a gift to others. In this spirit, the giver should respect the rights and liberties of the recipient.

**In governance, this means:**
- We protect space for authentic voices
- We don't prescribe how people should create
- We encourage experimentation
- We balance expression with community care

---

## Communal Effort

Our community values creative cooperation and collaboration. We strive to produce, promote and protect social networks, public spaces, works of art, and methods of communication that support such interaction.

**In governance, this means:**
- We build things together
- Our infrastructure serves community connection
- We protect shared spaces and resources
- We choose cooperation over competition

---

## Civic Responsibility

We value civil society. Community members who organize events should assume responsibility for public welfare and endeavor to communicate civic responsibilities to participants.

**In governance, this means:**
- We take our legal obligations seriously
- We're accountable to our community
- We communicate clearly about expectations
- We steward public trust responsibly

---

## Leaving No Trace

Our community respects the environment. We are committed to leaving no physical trace of our activities wherever we gather. We clean up after ourselves and endeavor, whenever possible, to leave such places in a better state than when we found them.

**In governance, this means:**
- We clean up organizational messes
- We don't create bureaucratic waste
- We sunset things that aren't working
- We leave things better than we found them

---

## Participation

Our community is committed to a radically participatory ethic. We believe that transformative change, whether in the individual or in society, can occur only through the medium of deeply personal participation.

**In governance, this means:**
- Spectators become contributors
- We create opportunities for involvement
- Decisions involve affected parties
- "Someone should" becomes "I will"

---

## Immediacy

Immediate experience is, in many ways, the most important touchstone of value in our culture. We seek to overcome barriers that stand between us and a recognition of our inner selves, the reality of those around us, participation in society, and contact with a natural world exceeding human powers.

**In governance, this means:**
- We value presence and direct experience
- Process serves action, not the reverse
- We cut through abstraction to real impact
- We don't bureaucratize wonder

---

## Using the Principles in Decision-Making

When evaluating proposals or making decisions, ask:

1. **Does this include or exclude?** (Radical Inclusion)
2. **Is this given freely?** (Gifting)
3. **Does this commercialize or commodify?** (Decommodification)
4. **Does this enable or create dependency?** (Radical Self-Reliance)
5. **Does this allow authentic expression?** (Radical Self-Expression)
6. **Is this a communal effort?** (Communal Effort)
7. **Are we being responsible stewards?** (Civic Responsibility)
8. **What's our cleanup plan?** (Leaving No Trace)
9. **How can people participate?** (Participation)
10. **Is this immediate and real?** (Immediacy)

Not every decision touches every principle, but all should align with the spirit.
